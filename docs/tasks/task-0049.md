# Log the Mazer multi-ligand model (2009) as a future cFT/multi-hormone candidate

Alex found and shared the Mazer Multi-Ligand Model (2009), also called
the "Mazer Spreadsheet Method" — a sixth-ish candidate for calculated
free testosterone, alongside Vermeulen, Ly & Handelsman, Sartorius,
Zakharov (see task-0044) and Nanjee-Wheeler (task-0046).

## The model, as described

Solves for 5 hormones — Testosterone, DHT, Estradiol, Estrone,
Cortisol — and 3 binding proteins — SHBG, Albumin, CBG — **simultaneously**,
using 15 association constants (one `K(protein-hormone)` pair per
hormone × protein combination). The free concentration fraction for
any one hormone X:

```
[Free_X] = [Total_X] / (1 + K(SHBG-X)·[Unbound_SHBG]
                           + K(Alb-X)·[ALB]
                           + K(CBG-X)·[Unbound_CBG])
```

Because each protein's unbound pool depends on all 5 competing
hormones at once, this cannot be written as a single closed-form line
the way Vermeulen's quadratic (`cft` in `indexDefs.ts`) or Ly &
Handelsman's (`cftlh`) can. It requires an iterative numerical solver
(e.g. Newton-Raphson) across the whole coupled 5-hormone/3-protein
system.

Checked via the PubMed MCP (`get_article_metadata`, PMID 19321131).
**This checks out as the genuine source** — title, abstract and
mechanism all match what Alex described:

> Mazer NA. "A novel spreadsheet method for calculating the free serum
> concentrations of testosterone, dihydrotestosterone, estradiol,
> estrone and cortisol: with illustrative examples from male and
> female populations." *Steroids* 2009;74(6):512-9.
> DOI: [10.1016/j.steroids.2009.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.steroids.2009.01.008)

The abstract explicitly confirms: 5 ligands (T, DHT, E2, E1/estrone,
cortisol) and 3 proteins (SHBG, albumin, CBG); "15 association
constants K(SHBG-X), K(Alb-X), and K(CBG-X)" estimated from a
systematic review of published binding studies; "an iterative
numerical method that can be readily programmed on a spreadsheet" —
matching Alex's "cannot be written as a single closed-form line, needs
Newton-Raphson-style iteration" description exactly. Unlike the
Zakharov screenshot (task-0044's 2026-09-15 update), this citation is
a direct PubMed metadata match, not a secondary reference that merely
name-drops the model — it checks out clean.

Full text was not pulled (metadata was sufficient to confirm identity
and mechanism); getting the actual 15 association constants and the
iteration procedure, if this is ever implemented, would need the full
text.

## Why this is a much bigger undertaking than another cFT badge

Flagging explicitly, since it would be easy to mis-scope this as
"add a sixth cFT variant" the way Nanjee-Wheeler (task-0046) was: it
is architecturally different from every other method in this repo.

- Every existing/candidate cFT method (Vermeulen, Ly & Handelsman,
  Sartorius, Zakharov, Nanjee-Wheeler) is **a single free-testosterone
  formula** — one hormone, one equation (closed-form or, for Zakharov,
  a still-unknown iterative one), taking T + SHBG (+ albumin) as
  inputs.
- Mazer is **a coupled 5-hormone equilibrium solver**. Computing even
  just free T from it means solving the whole system, because SHBG's
  and albumin's unbound pools depend on DHT, E2, E1 and cortisol too —
  it cannot be partially implemented for T alone without approximating
  the other four away.
- It would need **DHT, Estradiol, Estrone and Cortisol** as additional
  inputs (DHT and Estradiol already exist as observations elsewhere in
  the app; Estrone and Cortisol do not appear to be tracked at all
  today) plus **CBG** (corticosteroid-binding globulin) as a third
  binding protein alongside SHBG and albumin — none of which the
  current `cft`/`cftlh` infrastructure in `indexDefs.ts` /
  `computedIndices.ts` touches.
- It would need a numerical solver (Newton-Raphson or equivalent)
  rather than the closed-form quadratic solves `cft`/`cftlh` use today
  — a different class of computation, not a new formula in the same
  shape.
