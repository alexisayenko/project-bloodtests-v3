# Add cFT (Nanjee-Wheeler) as a fifth calculated free-T variant

Alex supplied a fifth calculated free-testosterone (cFT) formula, not
previously tracked anywhere in this repo:

**Nanjee-Wheeler Equation (1985)** — an empirical log-linear
regression that omits albumin entirely:

```
ln([FT]) = 1.053 * ln([TT]) - 1.051 * ln([SHBG]) - 2.82
```

Note: all hormone inputs (TT, SHBG) must be in nmol/L for the
natural-log outputs to scale correctly.

Source: <https://www.endocrine-abstracts.org/ea/0010/ea0010p76>

## Why this is a genuinely new addition

This is a **fifth** cFT variant, not a fill-in for an existing
"unavailable" slot. Vermeulen (`cft`) and Ly & Handelsman (`cftlh`)
are already implemented; Sartorius and Zakharov were tracked in
`HormonalPathwaysView.tsx`'s `BADGES` array as `unavailable: true`
placeholders (no formula implemented / paywalled coefficients /
independently reported inaccurate) until 2026-09-15, when both were
removed outright as permanent dead ends rather than left as stubs
(task-0044's "cFT-Z (Zakharov)" and "Sartorius removed too" updates).
No `unavailable: true` badge is left in the column at all today.
Nanjee-Wheeler never had an existing placeholder either way — it needs
a brand-new badge slot added to `BADGES`, not an `unavailable` flag
flipped.

Unlike Vermeulen (needs T, SHBG, albumin) and Ly & Handelsman (needs
T, SHBG only, two branches split on T), Nanjee-Wheeler is the
**simplest** of the three: one formula, two inputs (T, SHBG), no
albumin, no branching.

## What the code says

`web/src/data/indexDefs.ts`'s current cFT-related lineup (exact keys,
confirmed by reading the file):

- `cft` — "Free testosterone (calculated, Vermeulen)", inputs
  `T`, `SHBG`, optional `ALB`, formula solves the Vermeulen
  equilibrium-binding quadratic.
- `cftlh` — "Free testosterone (calculated, Ly & Handelsman)", inputs
  `T`, `SHBG` only, two-branch empirical polynomial split on
  T ≥ 5 nmol/L.
- `biot` — "Bioavailable testosterone", also built on the Vermeulen
  quadratic (free T × (1 + Ka·albumin)).
- `fai` — "Free androgen index", the older raw-ratio (T/SHBG × 100)
  alternative, explicitly described in its own `consensus` text as
  inferior to cFT.

Each is an `IndexDef` with `fn`, `references`, `evidenceLevel`,
mirrored by golden-master tests in `web/test/computed-indices.test.ts`
(e.g. `cft`'s and `cftlh`'s own `describe` blocks). A Nanjee-Wheeler
`cftnw` (or similar key) would follow the same shape: `fn` computing
the log-linear regression, `references` citing the abstract above,
`evidenceLevel` graded appropriately (an unreplicated 1985 conference
abstract, not a peer-reviewed journal formula like Vermeulen or
Ly & Handelsman — this task doesn't decide the grading, just flags
that the source is thinner than the other two).

## Done (2026-09-15)

Implemented as `cftnw` in `web/src/data/indexDefs.ts` (`IndexDef`,
between `cftlh` and `biot`) and wired into
`HormonalPathwaysView.tsx`'s `IndexKey` type, `INDEX_KEYS`, and
`BADGES` (id `cft-nanjee-wheeler`, between `cft-ly-handelsman` and
`tlh`). `evidenceLevel`/`level`: `'heuristic'`, the lowest of the
three cFT badges — the defining 1985 paper (Nanjee MN, Wheeler MJ,
*Ann Clin Biochem*, PMID 4041156) validated the method in hirsute and
normally-menstruating women, not men; the coefficients used here come
from an unreplicated 2005 SFE conference abstract (ea0010p76)
re-applying it to 116 healthy men, never published as a full
peer-reviewed paper.

Output unit was not stated explicitly in any source this session
could reach (both are paywalled/bot-blocked). Chosen as **nmol/L** by
the task's own "numerically tiny" sanity check: T=20 nmol/L, SHBG=30
nmol/L gives exp(1.053·ln(20) − 1.051·ln(30) − 2.82) ≈ 0.0392, not
"hundreds". That check could **not** be reconciled against the 2005
abstract's own reported finding that this equation runs ~14–20%
*above* Vermeulen in men (13.8±7.8% vs Sodergard, 19.9±7.4% vs
Vermeulen) — plugging typical adult-male T/SHBG into the transcribed
formula instead lands roughly an order of magnitude *below* cft/cftlh
at the same inputs. This discrepancy is documented in-app (`cftnw`'s
own `consensus` text and the badge's `caveats`) rather than resolved;
flagging it for whoever next has paywalled access to the primary
sources.

Because every `IndexDef` must carry a band
(`computed-indices.test.ts`'s "every index has a band to judge by"
invariant), `cftnw` reuses cft/cftlh's `cut: [100, 65]` — the same
quantity, same pg/mL scale — rather than going band-less; the
meaning/consensus/caveats text warns explicitly that, given the
magnitude mismatch above, this badge should be expected to read "low"
for a physiologically normal man and that should be read as a
probable scale artifact, not a real signal.

Verified: `npx vitest run` 891 passed / 1 skipped (892 total, up from
884/885 — 7 new tests: 2 hand-verified worked examples, a
missing-input null check, a zero/negative-input domain guard, an
input-shape check, and a bands check, plus the golden-master fixture
entry). `npm run build` (`tsc -b && vite build`) clean. Visually
confirmed in the dev server against the showcase dataset: badge shows
"cFT (Nanjee & Wheeler) 6.79 pg/mL" with a red/bad dot (T=16.1 nmol/L,
SHBG=39.2 nmol/L that draw), expands to show the reference bands,
Meaning, Low, High, Caveats and both Sources correctly.

## Update 2026-09-15 (reverted)

Reverted in full. The formula transcribed above — `ln(FT) = 1.053·ln(TT)
− 1.051·ln(SHBG) − 2.82` — was implemented from an AI chat tool's
screenshot, not read directly from a primary or verified secondary
source. Follow-up research after shipping found three independent
peer-reviewed papers applying Nanjee-Wheeler to men — Ho et al. 2006
(*Clinical Endocrinology*, n=126), Morris et al. 2004 (*Clinical
Endocrinology*, n=1072) and Sartorius et al. 2009 (*Annals of Clinical
Biochemistry*) — all finding the equation should read on the same scale
as Vermeulen, slightly higher, consistent with the 2005 SFE abstract's
own ~14–20% figure already quoted above. This app's implementation of
the transcribed formula instead computed values 6-8x LOWER than
Vermeulen for the same inputs — not a borderline discrepancy but a clear
sign of a wrong coefficient or a wrong unit somewhere in the
transcription, exactly the failure mode this task's own "Meaning" and
"consensus" text had already flagged as unresolved before shipping.

The primary 1985 paper (Nanjee MN, Wheeler MJ, PMID 4041156) and every
paper found that quotes its exact equation verbatim remain paywalled, so
there is no way to correct the formula right now. Reverting rather than
shipping a number already proven wrong, or hand-calibrating an output
unit/coefficient to "look right" against Vermeulen — that would just be
inventing a number, not implementing Nanjee-Wheeler.

Reverted: the `cftnw` `IndexDef` and its two dedicated helper functions
in `web/src/data/indexDefs.ts`; the `cftnw` badge, `IndexKey` union
member and `INDEX_KEYS` entry in
`web/src/components/conditions/HormonalPathwaysView.tsx`; the `cftnw`
golden-master entry and its dedicated `describe` block in
`web/test/computed-indices.test.ts`. `CLAUDE.md`'s badge-column
paragraph already read "eight badges" with no Nanjee-Wheeler mention, so
no change was needed there.

What it would actually take to implement this correctly: the real
printed equation from PMID 4041156 itself, or from a paper that quotes
it verbatim (not just cites it or reports a re-derived coefficient) —
institutional/interlibrary access to the 1985 *Annals of Clinical
Biochemistry* paper, or to a fully-accessible copy of the 2005 SFE
conference abstract (ea0010p76) that states the equation's output unit
explicitly, would resolve both the coefficient-transcription question
and the unit ambiguity this task's own text already flagged. Until then
this stays unimplemented rather than shipped wrong.
