# Implement the Martin-Hopkins LDL-C estimate

Goal: a third calculated LDL-C in `web/src/data/indexDefs.ts`, beside
the Friedewald and Sampson estimates being added now. Martin-Hopkins
was split out into its own task because its adjustable factor is a
180-cell lookup table that wasn't available in machine-readable form
when the other two were written.

Method: `LDL-C = TC − HDL-C − TG/F` (mg/dL), where `F` is a
strata-specific median TG:VLDL-C ratio selected by the sample's own TG
and non-HDL-C — not the constant 5 of Friedewald. That is the whole
method: the adjustable factor is why it beats Friedewald at high TG
and low LDL-C, so shipping it with a fixed factor would ship nothing.

Source: Martin SS, Blaha MJ, Elshazly MB, et al. *Comparison of a
novel method vs the Friedewald equation for estimating low-density
lipoprotein cholesterol levels from the standard lipid profile.* JAMA.
2013;310(19):2061–2068. DOI
[10.1001/jama.2013.280532](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.280532),
PMID [24240933](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24240933/).

Unlike Friedewald, Martin-Hopkins has LOINC codes of its own, both
naming the method: **96259-7** (mass/volume) and **96258-9**
(moles/volume). The `loinc` on the index def takes 96259-7, since
`INDEX_DEFS` computes in mg/dL.

Verification already done — don't redo it:

- 30 rows × 6 columns = 180 cells.
- TG strata contiguous from 7 to 13 975, no gaps and no overlaps.
- Every row non-increasing left to right.
- Exactly one column-wise dip: TG 97–100 at ≥220 non-HDL-C, 4.4 → 4.3.
  These are strata medians, so that reads as genuine empirical noise
  rather than a transcription slip.
- Values span 3.1–11.9, matching the published range.

## Open questions — decide before shipping, don't resolve in passing

- **Licensing.** The table was transcribed from a figure published
  under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 ([ResearchGate, "Median TG:VLDL-C ratios
  disaggregated by non-HDL-C and TG levels, 180-cell
  table"](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Median-TGVLDL-C-ratios-disaggregated-by-non-HDL-C-and-TG-levels-180-cell-table_fig1_325303245)).
  This repo is public, NC and SA are both viral, and monetization is
  still an open product question. The reasonable position is that the
  numbers are uncopyrightable facts and re-expressing them as JSON is
  not reproducing the figure — embedding the image would be. But that
  is a judgement call for the owner to make deliberately, and EU
  database rights are less clear-cut than US fact-copyright.
- **Where the table lives.** It fits the existing pattern exactly:
  reference data as JSON under `web/public/data/`, a closed-object
  schema under `web/public/schema/`, ajv validation in
  `web/test/reference-data.test.ts` — the shape
  [ADR-0010](../tech/decisions/adr-0010-analyte-catalog-is-the-source-of-truth.md)
  and
  [ADR-0011](../tech/decisions/adr-0011-molar-masses-are-data-factors-are-derived.md)
  already set for the analyte catalog and the molar masses.
- **Test design.** A strict monotonicity assertion would be wrong —
  the one genuine dip fails it, and hard-coding an exception hides
  the next real typo. Check trend plus outlier instead: each column
  rising overall, no cell deviating implausibly from its neighbours,
  plus the structural assertions (180 cells, contiguous strata,
  rows non-increasing).
- **Consistency with `vldl`.** The existing VLDL-C index is fixed
  Friedewald, TG ÷ 5. If Martin-Hopkins lands, LDL-C and VLDL-C would
  rest on contradictory assumptions about the same lipoprotein pool;
  open question whether `vldl` should move to the adjustable factor
  too.
